10 Reasons You Should NOT Buy That House

Everyone in the process is paid when you buy. That means almost no one is incentivized to talk you out of the wrong house — and the wrong house can cost you for a decade.

Here are ten honest reasons to walk away. A good agent will point these out before you fall in love; a desperate one will stay quiet.

1. The price per square foot is well above the neighborhood

You can love a home and still overpay. If it's priced far above comparable sales nearby, you'll fight that gap again when you resell.

2. There are foundation or structural cracks

In Texas clay soil, foundation movement is the expensive one. Never waive the structural inspection to win a bidding war.

3. It has been listed, pulled, and relisted repeatedly

A property that keeps coming back is telling you something the photos won't. Ask why every previous deal fell through.

4. There are non-permitted additions

That “bonus” converted garage or added room can become a legal and insurance headache. Confirm permits before you fall for the square footage.

5. It sits in a flood plain or has drainage problems

Hill Country runoff is real. Check the FEMA maps and look for water staining, grading issues, and hopeful landscaping hiding a problem.

6. The HOA is underfunded or in litigation

You inherit the HOA's finances and its lawsuits. Read the budget and meeting minutes before you're bound to them.

7. It's the most expensive house on the street

The block sets a ceiling on your value. The nicest home on a modest street rarely appreciates like a modest home on a great street.

8. Staging is hiding deferred maintenance

Beautiful furniture, tired systems. Look past the styling at the roof, HVAC, water heater, and windows — the things that cost real money.

9. You're rushing because of FOMO

Fear of missing out is the most expensive feeling in real estate. A home you're pressured into today is the regret you finance for thirty years.

10. Your agent won't mention any of the above

This is the biggest red flag of all. If the person guiding you never points out a downside, they're working for the commission — not for you.

A great home purchase should feel clear, not frantic. If you want an advisor who will genuinely tell you when to walk away — and knows the San Antonio and Hill Country market cold — that's exactly what we do.
