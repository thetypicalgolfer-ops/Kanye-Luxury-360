7 Things Your Realtor Will Never Tell You

Real estate runs on a few quiet truths that rarely make it into the sales conversation. None of them are scandalous — they're just things it's not in most agents' interest to volunteer.

Here are seven. Knowing them puts you back in control of your own deal.

1. The list price is often a marketing number, not the market's number

A price can be set to spark a bidding war, to flatter a seller, or to move fast — not to reflect true value. Always separate the asking price from the evidence.

2. “Motivated seller” language quietly leaks your negotiating power

The words in a listing and in conversation signal how hard someone will hold. A careful agent guards those signals; a careless one gives them away.

3. Dual agency can cost you thousands

When one agent or brokerage represents both sides, someone's leverage gets softened. Ask directly how your interest is protected.

4. A “new” listing may just be a reset

Pulling a stale listing and reposting it resets the days-on-market counter. It's a tactic, not a fresh opportunity — check the history.

5. Open houses mostly feed the agent's pipeline

They generate buyer leads for the agent more reliably than they sell your specific home. They're not useless — just understand who they really serve.

6. Commission is negotiable — and structure matters more than the percentage

How the fee is arranged, and what marketing it actually funds, matters more than the headline number. Ask what your money buys.

7. The best listings often sell off-market before you ever see them

A large share of premium homes trade quietly, through networks, before they hit the public sites. If you're not connected to that network, you're seeing the leftovers.

None of this means real estate is a trap — it means you deserve an advisor who says these things out loud. We'd rather earn your trust with candor than win a signature with a pitch.
